> **Key Point:** The week has the same phases as the day. Highs and lows of the week tend to form early (Mon to Wed) and the real move develops mid-week.

## Why profile the week at all

A daily bias only tells you direction. A range profile tells you *when in the week* that direction is likely to actually deliver, and roughly where the extremes will sit relative to Monday's range. ICT's mentorship material describes about a dozen recurring weekly patterns; you don't need to memorize all of them, you need to recognize the handful of families below well enough to say, by Tuesday or Wednesday, "this looks like X" — and adjust size and patience accordingly.

## Weekly profile families

| Profile | Structure | Trade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classic Tuesday | Monday holds a narrow range near the open; the manipulation (sweep of Monday's high or low) happens Tuesday in London or NY, then price reverses and trends the rest of the week | Wait for the Tuesday sweep before committing size, then trade the reversal direction into Thu/Fri |
| Classic Wednesday | Monday and Tuesday both trend or hold in one direction (often mistaken for the real move); Wednesday delivers a sharp sweep of the early-week extreme and reverses | Treat Mon/Tue direction as suspect until Wednesday confirms or invalidates it |
| Consolidation → Thursday reversal | Mon–Wed compress into a range; Thursday breaks with displacement and MSS, Friday continues or retraces slightly | Reduce size Mon–Wed, wait for the Thursday break to commit |
| Midweek expansion | Trend begins Monday/Tuesday, expands hard Wed–Thu on displacement, Friday consolidates or gives back part of the move | Trade pullbacks with bias once the Wed/Thu expansion confirms |
| Seek and destroy | Choppy, two-sided week with no single clean directional theme — both weekly highs and lows get run before Friday | Lowest-quality week type; cut size, fewer entries, require extra confirmation |

Whichever family a given week turns into is only obvious in hindsight by Wednesday–Thursday. The practical use is Bayesian, not predictive: each day that passes without the "expected" sweep/reversal narrows down which profile you're actually in, and you size accordingly — small early in the week while it's still ambiguous, larger once a profile confirms.

## Daily behavior inside the week

- London often sets the high or the low of the day (see Sessions & Killzones) — rarely both.
- NY AM distribution typically moves *away* from whichever extreme London set, toward the day's actual draw on liquidity.
- If London trended, NY often continues that move after retracing into the London FVG/order block first.
- If London consolidated (no clean high or low set), NY AM is more likely to take one side of that range and reverse the other way — effectively London and NY AM split the accumulation/manipulation/distribution roles between them.

## Worked example: a full week on MNQ

- **Monday:** narrow range, 19,700–19,760, no real displacement either direction. Daily bias: neutral, watching for Tuesday.
- **Tuesday:** London sweeps Monday's low into 19,680, reverses with displacement through 19,760 and holds above it into the NY close. This is now reading as a Classic Tuesday low — bullish bias for the rest of the week, invalidation below 19,680.
- **Wednesday:** NY AM pulls back into the Tuesday FVG, holds, and expands to 19,850. Confirms the Classic Tuesday read; this is now also a midweek expansion day.
- **Thursday:** continuation to 19,920, some give-back into the close as early profit-taking starts.
- **Friday:** reduced size after the NY AM open; price chops in a tightened range and doesn't take out Thursday's high — normal end-of-week behavior, not a reason to fade the week's bias.

## Rules

- The weekly high or low usually forms by Wednesday. Once one side is set, expect price to eventually reach for the *other* side of the week's eventual range.
- Friday: reduce size after the NY AM window — retracements and low-conviction chop are common as the week's participants de-risk into the weekend.
- Log the weekly profile you think you're in during Daily Prep, and grade it against what actually happened in the Weekly Recap. This is the fastest way to get calibrated on reading profiles live instead of only in hindsight.
