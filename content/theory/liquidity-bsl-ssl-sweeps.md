> **Key Point:** Price moves from liquidity to liquidity. Every trade needs a liquidity level as its trigger (what got swept) and a liquidity level as its target.

## Pools
- **BSL (buy-side liquidity):** above swing highs, equal highs (EQH), PDH, PWH, session highs, trendline highs. Sits on stop orders of shorts and breakout buys.
- **SSL (sell-side liquidity):** below swing lows, equal lows (EQL), PDL, PWL, session lows.

## Ranking (strongest first)
1. Weekly / monthly highs and lows
2. PDH / PDL, previous session highs and lows
3. Equal highs / equal lows
4. Recent swing highs and lows on the entry timeframe

## Sweep vs run

| Behavior | Read | Trade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wick through, body closes back inside | Sweep (raid). Reversal likely. | Wait for MSS, enter retrace, target opposite pool. |
| Body closes beyond and holds with displacement | Run (acceptance). Continuation. | Enter pullback in trend direction, target next pool. |

## Rules
- Equal highs/lows are targets, not resistance/support. Expect them to be taken.
- The more touches, the more stops are resting, the more likely a sweep.
- A sweep with a fast MSS and FVG = A-grade trigger. A sweep with slow grind back = wait.
- Target the **first** untouched pool in the trade direction. Only the next one after that if partials are taken.
- Turtle soup: sweep of an old high/low, then close back and reversal. Same as a sweep entry, trade it with MSS confirmation.
- Draw on liquidity is the decision: price is heading to the pool that is closest and least defended.
